If three [whistleblowing software](/) demos look identical, the problem may be the demo script. Most vendors can show a reporting form and a case dashboard. A useful evaluation makes each platform handle the same difficult situations and asks the vendor to show exactly what the system records, restricts and automates.

**Direct answer:** Ask vendors to demonstrate a complete sensitive case, not just the front-end form. Test anonymity, access restrictions, conflicts of interest, evidence, deadlines, audit history, language support, data location, integrations and contract exit. The questions below help expose the difference between a feature that exists and a feature your organization can actually use.

---

## Fifteen questions to ask every vendor

1. **Can you submit a report without creating an employee account?** Show the actual employee experience on a mobile browser.
2. **What information could identify a reporter?** Explain IP logging, device data, cookies, authentication, email notifications and operational logs. Provide the relevant technical documentation.
3. **Can anonymous reporters receive and send follow-up messages?** Demonstrate the exact workflow, including how access is recovered and how identity is protected. If the product does not support this, say so clearly.
4. **Who can view a sensitive report?** Show role permissions, entity restrictions and the difference between a platform administrator and an investigator.
5. **What happens if the report names the HR Director or system administrator?** Demonstrate independent escalation and access restrictions.
6. **How are acknowledgement and feedback deadlines tracked?** Show reminders, ownership and overdue-case escalation.
7. **How is evidence stored?** Demonstrate file access, activity history, export and retention controls.
8. **Can you show the audit history?** Identify which actions are recorded, who can view the log and whether entries can be changed or deleted.
9. **How do you support multiple legal entities and countries?** Show how reports can be routed without exposing unrelated cases to local teams.
10. **Which languages are supported end to end?** Test the reporting form, system messages, administrator experience, help content and support model.
11. **Where are production data, backups and support systems located?** Provide the subprocessor list and explain international access or transfers.
12. **What security evidence can we review?** Ask for the data processing agreement, security overview, incident process, penetration-test summary or independent certification where available.
13. **Which integrations are included?** Ask whether employee identity data is imported, why it is needed and how the integration affects anonymous submissions.
14. **What is included in implementation and support?** Request milestones, customer responsibilities, training scope, service levels and additional fees.
15. **How do we export and delete our data when we leave?** Ask for sample exports, format details, timelines, backup expiry and any exit charges.

## Run three scenarios during the demo

**Scenario A: A report about a senior leader.** Ask the vendor to show who receives it, how a conflict is detected and how access is restricted.

**Scenario B: A report from an employee in another country.** Test language options, local routing, entity-level access and the information shown to the reporter.

**Scenario C: A report that cannot be substantiated.** Ask how the investigator records evidence, explains the conclusion, tracks corrective actions and applies retention rules.

Use the same scenarios for every vendor. Ask the salesperson not to skip a difficult step. If a feature requires a custom project, record it as a dependency rather than scoring it as available today.

## Score the demo, not the presentation

Give each criterion a status: demonstrated, documented but not demonstrated, roadmap, custom work or unavailable. Record the evidence and the person responsible for verifying it. Involve HR, Compliance, Legal, IT Security and procurement, but keep sensitive case details out of the procurement process.

A strong vendor demo should make the product's limits visible. Clear limitations are safer than broad promises that cannot be verified after purchase. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is the most important whistleblowing software demo test?
Test a report involving a senior leader or administrator. This quickly reveals whether the platform supports independent escalation and meaningful access restrictions.

### Should IT Security attend the demo?
Yes, especially for questions about identity data, logs, hosting, subprocessors, authentication, encryption, audit records and incident response.

### Can a demo prove legal compliance?
No. A demo shows product behavior. Legal compliance also depends on local law, policy, operating procedures, contracts and how the organization uses the platform.
