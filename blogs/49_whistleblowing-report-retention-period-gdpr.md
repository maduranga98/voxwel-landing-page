Whistleblowing reports can contain sensitive allegations, personal information, witness statements and investigation records. Keeping them forever increases privacy and security risk. Deleting them too early can undermine an investigation, legal obligation or the organization's ability to explain what it did.

**Direct answer:** There is no single retention period that applies to every whistleblowing report in every country. Under the GDPR, personal data should be kept no longer than necessary for the purposes for which it is processed, subject to applicable legal obligations and other lawful grounds. Organizations should define a documented, case-sensitive retention schedule and review it regularly.

---

## What the GDPR says about storage limitation

Article 5(1)(e) of the GDPR establishes the storage limitation principle. Personal data should be kept in a form that permits identification only for as long as necessary for the purposes of processing. Longer retention may be possible in specified circumstances, such as archiving in the public interest or certain research and statistical purposes, subject to safeguards. A company should not use a generic "keep everything forever" rule for convenience.

The EU Whistleblowing Directive also addresses records. Article 18 requires reports to be recorded in compliance with confidentiality requirements and stored no longer than necessary and proportionate to comply with the Directive or other EU or national law.

These principles do not produce one universal number of months or years. The appropriate schedule depends on the nature of the case and the employer's legal environment. For the wider picture, see our [GDPR whistleblowing compliance](/blogs/gdpr-whistleblowing-compliance) guide.

## Separate the records you hold

A retention schedule should distinguish between:

- The original report and submitted attachments.
- Acknowledgements and communications with the reporter.
- Triage notes and assignment records.
- Interview notes and witness statements.
- Evidence gathered from systems or documents.
- Investigation findings and decision records.
- Corrective actions and monitoring records.
- Audit logs and access history.
- Backups, exports and temporary copies.

Different record types may have different purposes and retention needs. Do not assume that deleting the main case file automatically deletes backups, exports or copies held by other systems.

## Build a case-sensitive retention schedule

For each record category, document its purpose, legal or operational basis, owner, retention trigger, review date, deletion method and any exception. Possible triggers include case closure, completion of an appeal, end of a legal hold or expiry of a statutory period. The organization should have local counsel review relevant employment, whistleblowing, limitation and record-keeping rules.

A serious fraud investigation may need different treatment from a report that was clearly outside scope and closed at triage. A case connected to litigation or a regulatory inquiry may require a legal hold. A legal hold should be documented, limited to the relevant material and reviewed when the reason for holding the data ends.

## What should whistleblowing software support?

Ask whether the platform can apply retention rules by case type, status or jurisdiction. Can authorized staff place a legal hold? Can they record why retention is extended? Can the organization delete content while preserving a minimal audit record where lawful? Does deletion reach attachments, backups and subprocessors? Can the organization export records before contract termination?

The software should help carry out the policy, but it cannot decide the legal retention period by itself. The employer remains responsible for determining the purpose and lawful retention requirements. Include these questions in your [vendor demo checklist](/blogs/whistleblowing-software-demo-checklist-questions) and your [whistleblowing policy](/blogs/whistleblowing-policy-template).

## Avoid two common mistakes

The first mistake is indefinite retention because a report might become useful one day. This can conflict with data minimization and storage limitation. The second is automatic deletion immediately after closure without checking legal obligations, appeals, ongoing investigations or legal holds.

Use a documented review process. Assign an owner, schedule periodic checks and record exceptions. Keep the policy understandable to case handlers so sensitive data is neither retained unnecessarily nor destroyed prematurely.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does GDPR specify a fixed retention period for whistleblowing reports?
No. GDPR storage limitation requires retention to be tied to purpose and necessity. Sector-specific, employment, national and litigation rules may affect the appropriate period.

### Can a company retain a report after an investigation closes?
Potentially, where a lawful and necessary purpose remains, such as a legal obligation or a properly documented legal hold. The reason and duration should be reviewed.

### Should anonymous reports be deleted sooner?
Not automatically. Retention should be based on the information, purpose and legal requirements, not simply on whether the reporter provided a name.
