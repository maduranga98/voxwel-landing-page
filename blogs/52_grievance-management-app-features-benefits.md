Workplace grievances are inevitable in organizations. Employees may have concerns about management decisions, workplace policies, unfair treatment, communication problems, working conditions, or conflicts with colleagues. What matters most is how an organization receives, manages, and resolves these concerns.

When grievances are handled through scattered emails, paper forms, spreadsheets, and informal conversations, important information can easily get lost. Employees may not know whether anyone is addressing their concerns, while HR teams struggle to track progress and maintain consistent records.

A **grievance management app** provides a structured digital solution for handling these issues. It allows employees to submit grievances, enables administrators to review and manage cases, and creates a more organized process for communication and resolution.

In this guide, we explore how grievance management apps work, their essential features, and how Microsoft's Marketplace Grievance Management App by Dhyey Consulting Services approaches workplace grievance management.

## What is a grievance management app?

A grievance management app is a digital platform that helps organizations receive, record, track, investigate, and resolve complaints or concerns raised by employees.

Instead of relying entirely on manual processes, employees can submit grievances through a structured digital form. Each submission can be recorded, assigned a reference number, reviewed by authorized personnel, and tracked through different resolution stages.

A typical grievance management system includes:

- Employee grievance submission forms.
- Complaint categorization and reference numbers.
- Administrator dashboards.
- Case status tracking.
- Communication between employees and administrators.
- Historical grievance records.
- Reporting and monitoring capabilities.

The main objective is to make the grievance-handling process more organized, transparent, and accountable.

## Why do organizations need a digital grievance management system?

Traditional grievance management methods often create problems that become more difficult as organizations grow.

### 1. Employee complaints get lost

When employees report problems through emails, verbal conversations, or paper forms, it becomes difficult to maintain a complete record. A digital system creates a centralized place to record submissions and monitor their progress.

### 2. Employees cannot track their complaints

Employees may submit concerns without knowing whether HR has received them or what happens next. A grievance tracking system gives employees a way to follow the status of their submissions and review available responses.

### 3. HR teams struggle to manage multiple cases

Managing numerous complaints through spreadsheets and email conversations increases administrative work. A centralized dashboard helps authorized administrators review open, ongoing, and resolved cases.

### 4. Accountability becomes difficult

Without proper documentation, organizations may struggle to establish who reviewed a complaint, what action was taken, and when the issue was addressed. Digital grievance systems can provide a more consistent record of case activity.

### 5. Workplace problems remain unresolved

When employees feel that their concerns are ignored, workplace dissatisfaction can increase. A structured reporting and follow-up process helps organizations identify problems earlier and take appropriate action.

## Essential features of a grievance management app

Not all grievance management applications offer the same capabilities. However, several features are particularly useful when selecting a solution.

### 1. Digital grievance submission

Employees need a simple way to communicate their concerns. A submission form should allow employees to select a grievance category, describe the issue, and attach supporting documents where appropriate.

### 2. Automatic ticket generation

Every grievance should have a unique reference number. For example, a system might generate a ticket such as TKT-001. This makes it easier for employees and administrators to identify and discuss specific cases.

### 3. Role-based access control

Grievance records can contain sensitive personal and workplace information. Role-based access control (RBAC) helps restrict system functionality and information access to authorized users. Organizations should also consider who can view, edit, assign, and close individual cases.

### 4. Case communication

Communication between employees and administrators is an important part of grievance resolution. An integrated messaging or chat interface can help both parties exchange information, request clarification, and provide updates without relying on disconnected email conversations.

### 5. Grievance history

A useful system should maintain records of previous grievances, administrative replies, and resolution statuses. This historical information helps employees understand previous submissions and assists administrators in reviewing recurring concerns.

### 6. Administrator dashboard

HR teams need an overview of the organization's grievance activity. A dashboard can display open grievances, in-progress cases, resolved complaints, grievance categories, case status filters and historical submissions. These views help administrators monitor their workload and identify cases that need attention.

### 7. Status management

A grievance management workflow should clearly indicate whether a case is open, under review, or resolved. Administrators should be able to update case statuses and communicate relevant changes to employees.

## How does a grievance management app work?

Although workflows differ between platforms, a typical digital grievance management process follows these stages.

1. **Employee submits a grievance.** An employee accesses the application and submits a complaint using the available form, providing relevant details and supporting information.
2. **The system records the submission.** The application records the grievance and generates a unique reference number. The submission becomes available to authorized administrators.
3. **Administrator reviews the case.** The administrator reviews the complaint and determines the appropriate next action. Depending on organizational procedures, the case may require additional information or escalation.
4. **Communication and follow-up.** The employee and administrator communicate to clarify the issue and provide relevant updates.
5. **Resolution and status update.** After taking appropriate action, the administrator updates the status. The system maintains the available historical records for future reference.
6. **Review and improve.** Organizations can review grievance patterns to identify recurring problems and opportunities to improve workplace policies and processes.

## Grievance management vs. whistleblowing: what is the difference?

Grievance management and whistleblowing are related, but they serve different purposes.

A grievance generally concerns an employee's workplace experience or treatment. Examples include disagreements with supervisors, unfair workload distribution, workplace conflicts, concerns about leave or working conditions, and problems with internal policies.

Whistleblowing usually involves reporting suspected wrongdoing that may affect an organization, employees, customers, or the public. Examples include financial fraud, bribery and corruption, regulatory violations, serious workplace safety violations, and misuse of company resources.

Some concerns, including harassment and discrimination, may fall into either process depending on their nature and applicable policies.

Organizations should establish appropriate procedures for routing complaints and whistleblowing disclosures. A general grievance system should not automatically replace a confidential whistleblowing channel. For sensitive reports, evaluate whether anonymous submissions, protected two-way communication, confidentiality controls, and appropriate investigation workflows are supported. ## How to choose the right grievance management software

Before investing in a grievance management application, organizations should evaluate several important areas.

- **Employee experience:** Is the application simple enough for employees to submit complaints without unnecessary steps?
- **Confidentiality:** Can sensitive information be accessed only by authorized personnel? Does the application support anonymous reporting where required?
- **Case tracking:** Can employees and administrators follow the progress of individual grievances?
- **Communication:** Does the platform provide a reliable way to exchange messages and request additional information?
- **Reporting:** Can administrators identify recurring problems and monitor resolution activity?
- **Security:** What authentication, permissions, encryption, backup, and data protection measures are implemented?
- **Integration:** Does the application work with the organization's existing Microsoft 365 or other business systems?
- **Scalability:** Can the solution support the organization's expected employee and case volumes?
- **Support and licensing:** What are the subscription costs, trial conditions, implementation requirements, and ongoing support arrangements?

A good grievance management app should fit the organization's actual procedures rather than forcing employees and HR teams into an unnecessarily complicated workflow.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the purpose of a grievance management app?
A grievance management app helps organizations collect, record, track, and resolve employee complaints through a structured digital process. It improves visibility, communication, and accountability.

### What are the most important features of grievance management software?
Important features include digital submission forms, automatic ticket generation, role-based access control, case communication, grievance history, administrator dashboards, and status tracking.

### Can employees track their submitted grievances?
Yes. Applications with employee-facing grievance history and status tracking allow employees to review previous submissions, available administrator responses, and case progress.

### Is a grievance management app the same as whistleblowing software?
No. Grievance management generally focuses on workplace complaints and employee concerns. Whistleblowing software focuses on reporting suspected misconduct, fraud, corruption, and other serious wrongdoing. Some systems support both, but their confidentiality and workflow requirements may differ.

### Is the Grievance Management App by Dhyey Consulting available on Microsoft Marketplace?
Yes. Dhyey Consulting Services Pvt. Ltd. lists its Grievance Management App on Microsoft Marketplace. It is built using Microsoft Power Apps and SharePoint and includes grievance submission, ticket generation, chat, history tracking, and an administrator dashboard.

### Does every grievance management app support anonymous complaints?
No. Anonymous reporting must be specifically supported by the application. Organizations should verify whether employees can submit concerns without identifying themselves and whether administrators can communicate with anonymous reporters without compromising their identity.

### Why should organizations move from manual grievance management to software?
Digital software centralizes complaints, makes tracking easier, supports consistent communication, and provides structured records. It can reduce administrative effort and help organizations respond more systematically.
