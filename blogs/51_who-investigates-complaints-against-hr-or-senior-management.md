An employee who wants to report misconduct by an HR Director may be told to contact HR. A concern about the Chief Compliance Officer may arrive in the compliance inbox. A complaint involving the CEO may be routed to someone who reports directly to that executive. These situations create a conflict-of-interest problem before the investigation even begins.

**Direct answer:** A complaint should be handled by a person or function that is independent of the individuals implicated and has authority to act. Depending on the organization, that may mean a board committee, an independent director, an external investigator, Legal counsel or another designated recipient. The reporting policy should define this route before a sensitive report arrives.

---

## Why ordinary reporting lines can fail

A reporting channel is not independent if the person named in a complaint can read it, reassign it, delay it or influence the investigator. Even when no improper action occurs, employees may reasonably fear that the process is controlled by the person they are reporting.

The organization should identify conflicts at intake and provide an alternative route. Employees should not have to guess whether their report will be sent back to the person named in it.

---

## Define an escalation route in advance

Create a conflict matrix for common scenarios:

- **Complaint about a line manager:** route to a different manager, HR or another authorized recipient.
- **Complaint about an HR representative:** route to a senior independent manager, Legal, an ethics function or an external investigator.
- **Complaint about the Compliance Officer:** route to an audit committee, board-level recipient or designated independent channel.
- **Complaint about the CEO:** route to the board chair, independent director or board committee under the organization's governance documents.
- **Complaint about a board member:** use an independent board process or external adviser, subject to corporate law and governance rules.

These are design examples, not a universal legal hierarchy. The right recipient depends on the organization's structure, ownership, applicable law and governance documents. Document the matrix in your [whistleblowing policy](/blogs/whistleblowing-policy-template).

---

## Separate receipt, investigation and oversight

Where practical, do not give one person unlimited control over a sensitive case. The person who receives a report may not be the person who investigates it. Another authorized person may review the process or approve closure. Segregation reduces the risk of a case being suppressed or mishandled.

The platform should support restricted permissions, case reassignment, documented reasons for access changes and an audit history. A system administrator should not automatically receive unrestricted access to every complaint simply because they manage user accounts.

---

## Consider using an external investigator

An external investigator may be appropriate when internal independence is difficult, specialist expertise is needed or the allegation concerns the highest levels of management. Before appointing one, define their scope, reporting line, confidentiality obligations, access to records, conflicts and deliverables.

External support does not remove the organization's responsibility to make decisions, protect employees and comply with legal obligations. The board or authorized body should retain appropriate oversight.

---

## Protect confidentiality and procedural fairness

A person accused of misconduct should not be treated as guilty before the evidence is reviewed. A reporter and witnesses should be protected from retaliation. The investigation should be impartial, documented and proportionate. Share information only with people who need it for the process and where disclosure is permitted.

For U.S. employers, the EEOC's employer resources recommend accessible complaint routes, confidentiality to the extent possible and prompt, thorough and impartial handling of harassment concerns. Other countries have their own legal requirements, so obtain local advice. See also our [workplace discrimination investigation guide](/blogs/workplace-discrimination-investigation-guide) and [employee complaint management](/blogs/employee-complaint-management) overview.

## Test the process before a crisis

Run a tabletop exercise in which the CEO, HR Director and Compliance Officer are each named in a report. Confirm who receives the complaint, who can access it, who appoints the investigator, who monitors progress and who approves closure. Update the policy and software configuration based on the results.

A whistleblowing process is credible only when it works for the most senior person named in a report. Independence should be designed into governance, not improvised after a complaint arrives.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can HR investigate a complaint against the HR Director?
It may create a conflict. The organization should use an independent recipient or investigator with no reporting or personal conflict involving the subject of the complaint.

### Should the board investigate a complaint about the CEO?
The board or an authorized independent committee may oversee the response, often with external investigative support. The exact process depends on corporate governance documents and applicable law.

### Can whistleblowing software solve a conflict of interest?
Software can route cases, restrict access and record decisions, but it cannot create independence by itself. The organization must appoint the right people and define an escalation policy.
