A software vendor may describe its product as "EU Whistleblowing Directive compliant". That phrase is not enough to establish that the platform meets the needs of a particular employer. The Directive sets requirements for reporting channels and follow-up, while each Member State implements the rules through national legislation. An organization must therefore assess both the product and the laws that apply to its entities.

**Direct answer:** Verify an EU whistleblowing platform by testing secure receipt of reports, confidentiality, access restrictions, impartial follow-up, acknowledgement and feedback workflows, record keeping, data protection and country-specific requirements. Do not treat anonymous submission as the only test of compliance.

---

## What the Directive requires from internal reporting channels

Directive (EU) 2019/1937 requires covered legal entities to establish internal reporting channels and procedures for follow-up. For private-sector legal entities, the general threshold is 50 or more workers, subject to exceptions and additional national rules. The Directive also allows certain private entities with 50 to 249 workers to share resources for receiving reports and carrying out investigations, without removing their confidentiality, feedback and follow-up responsibilities.

Article 9 describes important workflow elements. Internal channels must be designed and operated securely to protect the identity of the reporting person and third parties named in a report from unauthorized access. The process includes:

- **Acknowledgement** of receipt within seven days
- **Designation** of an impartial person or department to follow up
- **Diligent follow-up**
- **Feedback** within a reasonable period not exceeding three months from acknowledgement, subject to the Directive's terms

The channel should also provide clear information about external reporting routes. A vendor should be able to demonstrate how your organization will configure these steps, not just display a submission form.

## Confidentiality is not the same as anonymity

This distinction matters. **Confidentiality** means the reporter's identity is restricted to authorized people and is not disclosed except under the conditions allowed by law. **Anonymity** means the reporter does not provide an identity, or the system is designed not to reveal it.

The Directive protects the confidentiality of the reporting person. It does not impose one universal rule requiring every organization in every Member State to accept and investigate anonymous reports. Article 6(2) leaves Member States room to decide whether organizations and competent authorities must accept and follow up anonymous reports, subject to other applicable EU law. National rules may therefore differ.

A platform should let the organization explain its local process accurately. Avoid describing a product as compliant solely because it has an anonymous form.

## The verification checklist

Ask the vendor to demonstrate each of these controls:

1. Secure intake through the channel types your policy requires.
2. Restricted access to reports and identity-related information.
3. Separate permissions for receiving, investigating and overseeing cases.
4. A process for acknowledging receipt within the required period.
5. Assignment to an impartial case handler or department.
6. Reminders and workflow controls for follow-up and feedback deadlines.
7. A controlled way to record evidence, actions and decisions.
8. Protection of third-party personal data mentioned in reports.
9. Record retention and deletion rules that can be configured and evidenced.
10. Clear information for employees about external reporting routes.

Ask the vendor to show what happens if the report names the person who normally administers the platform. If an accused executive or HR leader can access, reassign or delete the report, the workflow needs an independent escalation route.

## Ask for evidence, not a badge

Request the data processing agreement, hosting details, subprocessor list, access-control documentation, security testing information and incident-response commitments. Ask how the vendor supports deletion and export at the end of the contract. If the vendor offers country-specific compliance templates, ask which local laws were reviewed and when.

The GDPR also requires organizations to apply data minimization and appropriate security measures. A platform may help with operational controls, but it cannot make an employer compliant by itself. Policies, trained case handlers, employee communication and consistent follow-up remain the employer's responsibility. ## Build a country-by-country compliance map

For each legal entity, document the applicable national law, reporting population, available channels, responsible function, acknowledgement and feedback process, anonymous-report rules, record retention and external authority information. Have local legal counsel review the map before launch and when laws change. ## Frequently asked questions

### Does the EU Whistleblowing Directive require anonymous reporting?
Not universally. The Directive requires confidentiality protections and leaves Member States room to decide whether organizations must accept and follow up anonymous reports, subject to other applicable EU law and national rules.

### Does buying compliant software make a company compliant?
No. The employer still needs appropriate policies, trained impartial handlers, clear employee information, secure procedures and diligent follow-up.

### What deadlines should a platform support?
The Directive includes an acknowledgement period of seven days and a feedback timeframe not exceeding three months for internal channels, subject to the conditions in Article 9 and national implementation.
